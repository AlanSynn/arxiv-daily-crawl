## Motion Authoring, Editing & Retargeting

- 2026-10-08, **PriMoRetarget: Humanoid Motion Transfer Through Prioritized Motion Retargeting**, Nicholas Ioannidis et.al., Paper: [@2610.12642](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12642)
> **Abstract:** Motion retargeting is a critical preprocessing step for transferring human motion data to humanoid robots. While existing methods are effective for single-character motions, interactions involving multiple characters and objects introduce additional contacts and spatial relationships that must be preserved. Retargeting is commonly formulated as a weighted multi-objective optimization problem, where conflicting objectives can require careful tuning of relative weights and can still compromise important motion characteristics. We introduce PriMoRetarget, a prioritized motion-retargeting framework that replaces relative objective weighting with an explicit priority hierarchy. Lower-priority updates are constrained to preserve higher-priority objectives, allowing critical characteristics such as foot placement and interaction contacts to take precedence over lower-priority pose objectives. The method is evaluated on four datasets spanning single-character motion, character-object manipulation, and character-character interactions. The resulting motions better preserve task-relevant interactions and provide reference trajectories that can be tracked more reliably than those produced by widely used retargeting methods.

> **Abstract:** Text-guided long-term human motion generation is essential for applications such as continuously operating digital avatars. Despite recent progress on short clips, existing models struggle with multi-segment instructions that involve anaphora, where constraints established in earlier segments must be preserved over time. In this paper, we introduce a novel task of long-term motion generation from such anaphoric compositions. To mitigate this, we present ELMA, an episodic long-term motion dataset with dense segment-level annotations that preserve cross-segment dependencies. Built via a fully automated collection and filtering pipeline, ELMA extracts high-quality, continuous 30-second clips from in-the-wild videos, capturing the episodic narratives necessary for long-range state modeling. Leveraging this dataset, we fine-tune state-of-the-art motion generation models to incorporate long-range context. Extensive experiments reveal fundamental shortcomings in current models when handling context-dependent instructions. We demonstrate that ELMA enables significant improvements in both global physical realism and semantic alignment, setting a robust new baseline for long-term motion synthesis.
- 2026-10-09, **Sonic-AMG: Streaming Human Motion Generation from Spatial Audio**, Jingcheng Ni et.al., Paper: [@2610.13300](http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13300)
> **Abstract:** Modeling human reactions to spatial audio requires understanding both what is heard and where it comes from. Prior work generates motion offline from complete acoustic and spatial input sequences. Humans, however, react to sounds without knowing what they will hear next. We present Sonic-AMG, to our knowledge the first framework for streaming human motion generation from spatial audio. The model encodes acoustic content and body-relative source geometry as separate tokens. Conditioned on these tokens, a causal transformer with a flow head generates continuous motion latents, from which a causal decoder reconstructs motion segments. Standard teacher-forced training derives motion histories and body-relative source locations from ground-truth motion, whereas streaming inference relies on generated motion for both, resulting in a training-inference mismatch. To reduce this history discrepancy, we introduce rehearsal training, which combines full-sequence teacher-forced supervision with supervised prediction along short autoregressive self-rollouts. To keep spatial conditioning consistent with generated motion, we propose spatial feedback, which recomputes body-relative source locations from generated root poses during both rehearsal training and inference. On SAM, Sonic-AMG achieves state-of-the-art FID and R-precision. It further supports real-time online generation of coherent reactions across sound events and source locations. Project page: https://jingchengni.com/sonic-amg/.

> **Abstract:** Under a fixed physical law, the visible geometry of a scene determines how motion must change. We ask how video generators realize this relationship. Paired interventions change one thing at a time: a local bump, the height of a barrier, the words of the prompt, the length of the clip.
